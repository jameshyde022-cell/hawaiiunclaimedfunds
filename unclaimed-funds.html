Unclaimed funds in Hawaii, explained

Every year, billions of dollars in forgotten money and financial assets are turned over to state treasuries. It legally belongs to the individuals, businesses and heirs who lost track of it.

Start a free claim review Search the State database

Where unclaimed property comes from

  • Uncashed checks

    Payroll checks, vendor payments or refund checks that were never deposited.

  • Bank accounts

    Forgotten checking, savings or certificate of deposit (CD) accounts.

  • Deposits

    Utility or rent security deposits left behind after a move.

  • Insurance and investments

    Unclaimed life insurance payouts, policy refunds, stocks or dividends.

  • Estates

    Inherited funds that family members were never told about.

Why the State still holds your money

When an account sits inactive for a few years, the business holding the money is legally required to turn it over to the State for safekeeping.

The State holds the money until the owner claims it, but it does not track owners down. Unless someone comes forward with proof of ownership, it stays there.

How I trace owners and heirs of unclaimed property

I run an independent, one-man asset recovery operation that audits public records and uncovers forgotten accounts. Finding the money is only the first step; tracking down the rightful owner takes real investigative legwork.

  • Business and corporate records

    DCCA filings and business registries, to find the current owners or officers of dissolved or relocated companies.

  • Address and skip-tracing searches

    Old addresses, property records and public data, to locate people who have moved over the years.

  • Genealogy and heir research

    Family trees and estate records, to locate rightful heirs when the original owner has died.
