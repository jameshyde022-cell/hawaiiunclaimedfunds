For Families & Trustees

Heirs, Executors & Successor Trustees

A large share of my letters go to the families of deceased owners and to successor trustees — not to the original owner. If that's you, here's what you're probably wondering.

❓

Am I Entitled to Claim?

If the original owner is deceased, the right to claim generally passes to their heirs, their estate, or — if the property was held in trust — the successor trustee. Whether you personally can claim depends on your relationship to the owner, whether there's a will or trust, and whether an estate has been probated. I'll walk through your specific situation with you at no cost.

📄

What Documents the State Requires

Typical requirements include a certified death certificate, proof of your relationship to the deceased, and proof of your legal authority to act — such as Letters Testamentary or Letters of Administration from probate court, a small-estate Affidavit for Collection of Personal Property, or a Statement of Succession and Chain of Title showing how the property passes to you. For trust property, the State typically requires the trust instrument or a Certificate of Trust naming you as successor trustee.

⚖️

How Estate & Trust Claims Differ

Ordinary claims just need to prove you're the person named on the record. Estate and trust claims need an extra layer of proof — that the named owner is deceased, and that you're legally authorized to claim on the estate's or trust's behalf. That usually means more documentation and a longer review, especially with multiple heirs or no formal probate. I handle this documentation regularly and will tell you plainly what your specific claim needs.

Which of these applies depends on the size of the estate and how the property was titled — a small-estate affidavit and letters testamentary are alternatives to each other, not steps in a sequence. I'll tell you which ones your claim actually needs.
