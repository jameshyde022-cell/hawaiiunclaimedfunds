More About Me

For Families & Trustees

Heirs, Executors & Successor Trustees

Many of my letters go to the families of deceased owners and to successor trustees — not to the original owner. If that's you, here's what you need to know.

❓

Am I Entitled to Claim?

If the owner is deceased, the right to claim generally passes to their heirs, their estate, or — for trust property — the successor trustee. It depends on your relationship to the owner, whether there's a will or trust, and whether the estate went through probate. I'll go through your situation with you at no cost.

📄

What the State Requires

Usually a certified death certificate, proof of your relationship to the owner, and proof of authority — Letters Testamentary or Administration, a small-estate Affidavit for Collection of Personal Property, or a Statement of Succession. For trust property, the trust instrument or a Certificate of Trust naming you as successor trustee.

⚖️

Why These Claims Take Longer

A regular claim only proves you're the person on the record. Estate and trust claims also have to prove the owner is deceased and that you're authorized to act. A small-estate affidavit and letters testamentary are alternatives, not steps in a sequence — I'll tell you which one your claim actually needs.
